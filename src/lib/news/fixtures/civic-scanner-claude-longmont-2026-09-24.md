### **LEAD 1: Council votes 4-3 to bring back marijuana hospitality, limited to venues that also sell**

**Score: 16/20** | Immediacy 4 · Impact 3 · Conflict 5 · Novelty 4 | **ADVANCE** | Beat: NEW

Under future agenda items on September 22 (0:23:37 to 0:36:25), Council Member Jake Marsing moved to direct the city manager and city attorney to bring back ordinance O-2025-37, the marijuana hospitality implementation ordinance, "on a timeline consistent with staff's work plan." His motion adds one change: the package would authorize only retail marijuana hospitality **and sales** licenses, and would amend Chapter 6.70 to remove hospitality licenses without a sales component. Council Member Alex Kalkhofer seconded. The chair announced the motion carried 4-3, with Council Members Diane Crist, Matthew Popkin, and Crystal Prieto opposed.

Marsing said the reason is revenue, not personal support. City Clerk Dawn Quintana (name spelling to confirm) said the ordinance is "basically drafted" but licensing forms and review work stopped when the ordinance failed, and asked for time until after the November election. Marsing said he understood that meant roughly the first quarter of 2027\.

The packet for the same meeting (page 819\) gives the fiscal context: Council allowed up to four hospitality licenses in January 2025 (O-2025-01), but O-2025-37 "died on second reading on June 3, 2025," so consumption is still not allowed and no licenses have been issued. Staff estimates license fees at up to \$8,000 a year for four venues, sales tax is unknown, and staff has "received minimal inquiries." The 2027 budget message says the State has eliminated its shared marijuana revenue to the city.

Opposition on the record: Prieto cited potency and safety in a social setting; Crist cited prior testimony on smoking in public places; Popkin said he had not finished his own review.

**Why it matters for Longmont:** This revives a policy that failed in 2025 on a 3-3 vote, now framed as a revenue tool, with staff's own estimate of direct fee revenue at \$8,000 a year.

*Sources: Sept 22 council recording 0:23:37 to 0:36:25 (transcript-based); Sept 22 packet p. 819 (Tier A); 2027 Budget Message, Sept 1 (Tier A, CONTEXT).*

**Plain-language version:** Longmont City Council voted 4 to 3 on Tuesday to bring back a plan that would let a few businesses host people who use marijuana on site. The plan failed in 2025\. This version would allow only businesses that also sell marijuana. Council Member Jake Marsing said the city needs new money because marijuana tax income has dropped. Three council members voted no. They raised concerns about safety, smoking, and how strong today's products are. City staff say the license fees would bring in at most about \$8,000 a year. Sales tax income is unknown. This vote does not approve anything yet. It only tells staff to bring the ordinance back. The city clerk asked to wait until after the November election, so it may return in early 2027\.

### **LEAD 2: Dry Creek annexation passes first reading with four council-added conditions**

**Score: 15/20** | Immediacy 5 · Impact 3 · Conflict 4 · Novelty 3 | **ADVANCE** | Beat: NEW (related to earlier Quail Road coverage)

O-2026-62 would annex about 7.95 acres at Clover Basin Drive and Airport Road and zone it Residential Mixed Neighborhood (R-MN), according to the packet (pp. 39 to 41). The concept plan caps density at 7 units per acre for all single-family detached, or 15 units per acre for attached and multifamily. The traffic study in the packet assumes a maximum of 120 homes and "a maximum of 864 daily trips" (p. 381). Planning and Zoning recommended approval 6-1 on December 17, 2025\.

Council Member Popkin pulled the item from consent (1:08:23). Council then voted on conditions (all transcript-based):

1. Implement the traffic impact study recommendations "to the maximum extent feasible" (Popkin/Marsing): unanimous (1:38:40).

2. On-site, publicly accessible bike share compatible with the city system, in the final multimodal plan (Popkin/McCoy): 6-1, Marsing opposed (1:59:52). The applicant's representative said this was "the first we've heard" of it and cost is unknown.

3. Traffic control to deter through traffic north on Box Elder Drive, including a physical deterrent and signage against right turns onto Quail Road (Popkin/Marsing): unanimous (2:07:13). The fire marshal said the fire code prohibits full speed bumps but a cushion design may be acceptable.

4. Silo preservation (Popkin/Crist): withdrawn by the maker (2:18:56). In its place, refer the on-site silo to the Historic Preservation Commission before preliminary plat (Marsing/McCoy): 6-1, Prieto opposed (2:24:29).

The ordinance as amended passed first reading unanimously (2:27:22). Second reading and the statutory public hearing are scheduled for October 6\. Staff said the neighboring Quail Road annexation (310 apartments) "has been withdrawn at this time I believe."

**Why it matters for Longmont:** It is the first annexation this council has shaped with conditions, at an intersection residents describe as congested, and at least one condition has an unknown cost to the developer.

*Sources: Sept 22 packet pp. 39 to 42, 76 to 93, 381 to 387 (Tier A); Sept 22 recording 1:21:23 to 2:27:22 (transcript-based); public comment 0:47:06 and 0:56:22.*

**Plain-language version:** A developer wants Longmont to annex about 8 acres of farmland at Airport Road and Clover Basin Drive and build a mix of houses, townhomes, and small apartment buildings. The traffic study counts up to 120 homes and up to 864 car trips a day. On Tuesday, the City Council gave first approval, but first added rules. The developer must follow the traffic study's advice, add a public bike share station, and build something that stops drivers from turning right into the quiet senior neighborhood to the north. Council also asked the city's historic preservation board to look at an old silo on the land. Neighbors spoke about traffic at the intersection. The final vote and a public hearing are set for October 6\.

### **LEAD 6: On-bill financing enabling ordinance passes first reading 5-2**

**Score: 13/20** | Immediacy 4 · Impact 3 · Conflict 3 · Novelty 3 | **ADVANCE** | Beat: NEW

O-2026-63 amends Chapter 14.20 to let the city "consider the design, implementation and funding mechanisms" of on-bill financing, where residential customers repay home efficiency or electrification upgrades through their utility bill. "No financing programs are being proposed with this ordinance" (packet p. 466).

Popkin pulled it from consent. Two amendments passed unanimously: rename "on-bill charge" to "on-bill improvement repayment" (Popkin/Kalkhofer, about 2:29:50) and limit eligible improvements to those "requested by the customer" (Popkin/Marsing, 2:31:40). Crist raised the ordinance's provision (section B3) that allows service disconnection for nonpayment of the repayment; she said Fort Collins caps loans at \$40,000. Staff (Susan Bartlett, LPC) said disconnection is the city's main collection tool and on-bill programs have low default rates; the city manager cited the charter bar on lending city credit. Prieto asked about staff capacity and said a third-party lender should not be able to trigger a disconnection. The ordinance as amended passed 5-2, Crist and Prieto opposed (Popkin/Kalkhofer, 2:47:05). Second reading is scheduled for October 6\.

**Why it matters for Longmont:** It creates a path to utility-bill loans, and the disconnection question will shape any program that follows.

*Sources: Sept 22 packet pp. 466 to 468 (Tier A); Sept 22 recording 2:27:35 to 2:47:30 (transcript-based).*

**Plain-language version:** Longmont may one day let homeowners and renters pay for things like insulation, heat pumps, or solar panels a little at a time on their city utility bill. On Tuesday, City Council gave first approval, 5 to 2, to a code change that allows the city to design such a program. No program exists yet. Two council members voted no. They worried that if a person falls behind on the loan payment, the city could shut off their power, as it can for any unpaid bill. Staff said shutoff is the city's main way to collect, and these programs rarely see missed payments. The final vote is set for October 6\.

**Plain-language version:** Longmont now has a new volunteer board to advise the City Council on technology. City Council gave final approval on Tuesday with no votes against. Residents who spoke asked the council to pick members who ask hard questions, not people who sell technology. One speaker asked that the board review the city's contract for police cameras and an AI report-writing tool. City staff expect the board to take 20 to 30 hours of staff time each month.
