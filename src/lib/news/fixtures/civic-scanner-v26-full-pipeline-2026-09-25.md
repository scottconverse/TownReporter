### S1 — Council approves on-bill financing for efficiency upgrades

**Score:** immediacy 4 / local impact 5 / conflict 2 / novelty 3 = **14, advance**. **Editorial readiness:** **Tier 1, ready for edit** as a government story; confirm the ordinance number and the effective date against the posted agenda before publishing. **Why it matters:** The change lets owners pay for insulation, heat pumps and wiring on their utility bill instead of up front.

**Draft (about 220 words).** Longmont's city council gave final approval to an on-bill financing program that lets property owners repay the cost of efficiency work through the utility bill rather than in one payment. The ordinance covers insulation, air sealing, heat pumps, and the electrical work those upgrades require, and it applies to both owners and, with the owner's consent, renters.

The program's administrator is Longmont Power & Communications, which already bills most addresses in the city. Participants sign a repayment agreement that follows the meter: the balance transfers with the property if it is sold, and the city has said participation is voluntary and tied to a credit check. The council set the interest rate in the ordinance rather than leaving it to staff.

Staff told the council the program responds to owners who want to cut energy use but cannot carry the full cost in one season. The council also asked for a report after the first year covering how many projects were completed, in which neighborhoods, and how many applicants were turned down.

The program begins after the ordinance is published. Owners should wait for the city's own program page and application before signing anything with a contractor.

**Plain-language summary (about 100 words).** The city council approved a program that lets owners pay for insulation, heat pumps, and related electrical work a little at a time on their utility bill. It is voluntary, and the city says the debt follows the property if it is sold. The city has not published the application page yet. Owners should wait for the city's own program page before signing a contract, and should check the interest rate and the credit-check rules in the ordinance.

### S2 — 21st Avenue rail crossing set for two closures

**Score:** immediacy 5 / local impact 4 / conflict 2 / novelty 3 = **14, advance**. **Editorial readiness:** **Tier 1, ready for edit** as a service story; reconfirm the dates on the project page before publishing. **Why it matters:** Drivers and pedestrians face different, extended restrictions at a busy crossing.

**Draft (about 210 words).** Construction at Longmont's 21st Avenue railroad crossing is scheduled to start Monday, Sept. 28, with two separate full closures to vehicles. The first is expected to run through about Oct. 9 while crews improve drainage and build curbs and medians. The crossing is then expected to reopen to vehicles for roughly 24 days before a second full closure from about Nov. 3 to Nov. 24, when BNSF completes rail safety work and crews build sidewalks.

The north-side pedestrian crossing is expected to remain closed throughout the work, including the interval when vehicles can use the road again. The city directs pedestrians to cross 21st Avenue at Meadow Drive or Olympia Avenue and use a temporary sidewalk on the south side to cross the tracks. Drivers will be detoured via Collyer Street, 17th Avenue and Alpine Street during the full closures. Trains will continue to use the tracks.

The city cautions that dates may shift with weather, railroad coordination, or unexpected conditions. Its project hotline is 720-526-2044.

**Plain-language summary (about 100 words).** The city plans to close the 21st Avenue railroad crossing to cars from Sept. 28 to about Oct. 9, reopen it for several weeks, then close it again from about Nov. 3 to Nov. 24. The north-side pedestrian crossing is expected to stay closed throughout construction. People walking should use the city's marked crossing and temporary sidewalk route; drivers should follow the posted detour. All construction dates are estimates.

### S3 — Students to prepare free evening meals for Longmont seniors

**Score:** immediacy 4 / local impact 3 / conflict 1 / novelty 3 = **11, advance**. **Editorial readiness:** **Tier 2, developing** — the partnership is announced and the dates are published, but the number of meals, any eligibility rule, and whether registration is required are all gaps the city has not filled. **Why it matters:** This is a concrete food-access service and a school-to-city partnership.

**Draft (about 210 words).** Longmont's Senior Center plans to begin offering free take-home evening meals on Friday, Oct. 2, through a partnership with St. Vrain Valley Schools' ProStart and Culinary Arts program. Students at the district's Career Elevation and Technology Center will make chilled dinners designed with seniors' dietary needs in mind, according to the city's Sept. 24 announcement. The same school program will also provide meals for young people in city Children, Youth and Families programming.

Meals will be available at 3 p.m. on select dates at the Senior Center, 910 Longs Peak Avenue. The city lists Fridays Oct. 2, 16 and 30; Nov. 13; and Dec. 11, plus Tuesdays Nov. 24 and Dec. 22. The schedule is generally every other Friday, with adjustments around school closures. Residents can call Senior Services at 303-651-8411 for current details before making a trip.

Senior Services Manager Ronnie Maynes says funding currently runs through the end of 2026 and the city is exploring ways to continue in 2027.

**Plain-language summary (about 95 words).** Beginning Oct. 2, the Longmont Senior Center says it will give out free evening meals for people to take home. Students in a St. Vrain Valley Schools culinary program will prepare the chilled dinners. Pickups are scheduled for 3 p.m. on several Fridays through December and two Tuesdays near holidays. The city says the current funding lasts through 2026. The announcement leaves some practical questions open, including the number of meals available and any eligibility or registration rules.
