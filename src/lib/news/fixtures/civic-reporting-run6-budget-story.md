# Longmont’s proposed budget trims energy work as transit funding needs emerge

Longmont’s proposed 2027 budget would cut funding for a distributed-energy project by $1.18 million, leaving $916,000, staff told City Council during its October 6 meeting. The change remains a proposed spending reduction; the available excerpts do not establish final budget enactment or what individual utility customers would experience.

Staff described the reduction while explaining funding needed for the First and Main transit station. That puts two public investments into the same budget discussion, but the precise financial relationship remains unresolved. The excerpts do not establish a dollar-for-dollar transfer from energy work to transportation.

The energy work was identified as project 103, distributed energy resources innovation and solutions. Staff said the revised electric and broadband fund proposal would contain $151.8 million in expenses. That figure describes proposed expenses, not a change in fund balance or an announced utility-rate increase.

Later, the chair announced that the budget-item motion carried unanimously. The supplied action account describes the motion as approving the proposed 2027 budget and proposed 2027–2031 capital improvement program with council direction. Its complete wording is absent from the raw excerpts, leaving final enactment and appropriation authority unconfirmed.

A separate budget-related motion also passed, with the chair announcing a 6–1 result. The preceding discussion addressed a subsidy phaseout moving to 75% and then 100%. One speaker acknowledged the argument for resolving rates and charges first, but said staff needed budget direction that night. Although the supplied action account describes an earlier airport-fee motion, the missing intervening discussion prevents securely attaching its original percentages and effective years to this vote.

Neighborhood safety also shaped a separate land-use decision. Council amended ordinance 2026-69 to require the property owner to remove or mitigate fuel loads and hazardous vegetation identified by the Longmont Fire Department and city forester by May 1, 2027, subject to environmental, nesting-season and permitting requirements. The chair explicitly announced that the motion to amend carried 5–2.

The amendment’s sponsor said it responded to residents’ concerns and sought mitigation sooner rather than leaving it to a lengthy development process. That explanation supports the purpose of the condition; it does not independently establish the property’s fire risk or guarantee the work’s effects.

The chair then announced unanimous passage of a second amendment, followed by approval of the ordinance as amended, 6–1, with Council Member McCoy opposing. The supplied action account places ordinance 2026-69 among first-reading items. The decision therefore should not be presented as final annexation adoption without confirming its reading designation and complete ordinance language.

Copper Peak was a separate concept-plan case. The supplied account identifies a motion to approve ordinance 2026-73, and the supplemental continuation provides the previously missing announcement: the chair said the motion carried 5–2. A further 5–2 announcement followed at 3:54:32, but its motion is absent. It cannot be treated as another Copper Peak decision.

The Copper Peak discussion exposed an unresolved question for people hoping to buy homes. A council speaker asked whether the residential portion could eventually become entirely rental multifamily housing. The responses did not identify a restriction. That exchange does not prove that the written plan lacks one.

The applicant’s representative described lower-density development toward the west and higher density toward the east across a broader project. The representative also said later stages would return through the planning commission and council, arguing that flexibility was needed as development details and market conditions evolved. Those statements describe the applicant’s position, rather than guaranteeing a housing mix or establishing a future hearing date.
