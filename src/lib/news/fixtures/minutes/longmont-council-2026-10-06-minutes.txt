Public participation at regular and special City Council meetings is regulated by Rule
5 of City Council Rules of Procedure.
• Speakers should sign up on the sheet outside of Council Chambers prior to
the start of the meeting at 7 pm.
• Each speaker is limited to three (3) minutes and one person may not give or
assign their time to others. The Chair may regulate the time for public
participation, may restrict cumulative or redundant presentations, and may
require that each speaker’s comments pertain to the subject under discussion

during “First Call – Public Invited To Be Heard” (Item 8) and must sign up on
the list prior to the start of the meeting
• Persons wishing to speak on a specific Second Reading or Public Hearing
Item (Item 10) are asked to add their name to the speaker list for the specific
item prior to the start of the meeting

Public Invited To Be Heard” (Item 13)

A. O-2026-63, A Bill For An Ordinance Amending Chapter 14.20 Of The Longmont
Municipal Code Concerning On-Bill Financing And Repayment For Eligible
Improvements (Amended on First Reading)
Mayor Hidalgo-Fahring read the title of the ordinance into the record. There
was no formal staff presentation on this item.
Council discussed with staff a concern about the potential for service
disconnects due to increased bills because of the on-bill financing and
repayment option and asked staff if they could be separated so that service
would not be disconnected for the portion of the bill that was not just
utilities.
Lance Smith, Director of Business Enablement, explained that staff
was proposing an opt-in program to finance projects creating efficiency in theCity Council Regular Session, October 06, 2026 Page 12
City Council Regular Session, October 20, 2026 Page 18
home such as insulation, HVAC equipment and such. Residents would select
to participate in this program but could go elsewhere to secure financing. The
program has favorable financing and would benefit households that might not
normally qualify. Smith further clarified that with this ordinance, staff is
seeking to develop a program but does not have one developed yet.
Mayor Hidalgo-Fahring opened a public hearing on this item.
John Lembke spoke in favor of this ordinance and said that the cost of electric
is driven up by peak demand and in order to lower rates, we have to shave
peak demand. This program could help with that, particularly for households
where the cost or financing is a barrier.
Marcia Martin encouraged council to vote for this and pointed out that
building envelope projects have been suggestions of the Sustainability
Advisory Board since before 2015 and not adopted. She said that the people
who need the work and could most benefit from it may not be able to afford
the upgrades.
Mayor Hidalgo-Fahring closed the public hearing.
