Longmont Board Posts Two Oct. 8, 2026 Records: A Funding Hearing Packet and a Cancellation Notice

The Housing and Human Services Advisory Board has a posted Oct. 1 funding hearing packet and an Oct. 8 funding hearing packet — and a separate posted notice cancelling its Oct. 8, 2026 meeting.

Save Share Aa Text size
In this article The story Sources & records Corrections Read next

Longmont's Housing and Human Services Advisory Board has posted a packet for Human Services Funding Hearings on Oct. 1, 2026, at 6 p.m., and a second packet for the same hearings on Oct. 8, 2026, at 6 p.m. Both are labeled "Agency Hearings" and both agendas list a call to order, a "Public invited to be heard" item, and the funding hearings.

A separate posted notice states that "the OCTOBER 8, 2026 meeting of the Housing and Human Services Advisory Board has been cancelled." That notice's agenda lists only a call to order, "Public invited to be heard," and adjournment.

So the board's public record carries two distinct Oct. 8, 2026 items — a funding-hearing packet and a cancellation notice. The posted pages do not say whether the cancellation applies to the funding hearing or to a separate regular board meeting.

Why it matters locally: these hearings are the dated public window in which agencies seeking 2027 human services funding present to the advisory board. Residents who want to follow how city human services dollars are allocated have specific evenings to track.

The Oct. 1 hearing is designated for Education agencies and the Oct. 8 hearing for Self-Sufficiency agencies, per the packets. The packets do not state whether public comment is taken during the agency presentations themselves.

The size of the 2027 funding pool, the criteria the advisory board uses to score agencies, and the decision timeline after the hearings are not stated in the pages reviewed.

For context, the city's Human Services Grant Program receives 3% of General Fund revenue; in 2024 that totaled roughly $2.6 million, split between safety-net services and funding to address housing insecurity and adult homelessness, according to Longmont Leader reporting on the council's approval of that year's allocation.
