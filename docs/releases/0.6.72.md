This release is **the redesign reaching the paper and the desk at the same
time, and three things an editor could not see before**. The front page and the
article page are re-laid out to the handoff; dark mode becomes one warm black
instead of two black-and-white products; a desk job stops being a spinner and
one overwritten sentence and becomes a card that names its stages, moves a bar,
says how long the model has been quiet, and can be cancelled; and one new
screen, **Models**, answers which model runs which job. **The local-model
picker recorded in the 0.6.71 guide ships live in this release too**: 0.6.71 was
published but never put live, so that work reaches the paper with this one.
**No model was loaded, unloaded, pulled or called to write this release or to
check it** — every check ran against fakes: fake catalogs, fake servers, a fake
clock, and PGlite.

## The paper, re-laid out

The front page is a bled masthead row, a yellow-ruled lead, a three-column
section, a "This week" panel printed as an ink block, a six-cell story grid
with section tags, an "Around the region" band, the opinion panel, a twelve-row
river, and an ink footer. The article page is a breadcrumb, section tag and
headline over a byline bar, then three tracks: a sticky "In this article" jump
list, the body with "How we reported this" as a grid of source cards, and an
aside carrying "Dates in this story".

Both pages were rebuilt **over the routes and data that already existed**.
Every route, every read, and every provenance and correction feature is still
there; what changed is the layout, the type scale and the surfaces.
Corrections, the share panel and "Keep reading" keep their own headings and
anchors (`#story-corrections`, `#related`), so every existing link still lands.

**A headline weight defect was fixed on the way.** The lead headline inherited
`font-weight: 500` from the reader's global rule and had no weight of its own,
so it computed a weight the scale does not use. It is now 800, and every
headline the paper prints was measured rather than assumed: 280 headline rows,
all in Bricolage Grotesque, every one 700 or 800.

## The geography pills finally filter

The pill row — Longmont · Nearby · Boulder County · Colorado — has been on the
paper since the first build and has never been able to filter anything, because
a printed story carried nothing that said which ground it stands on. It does
now, and the tag behind it is decided: **a story with no area counts as the home
town (Longmont)**, which keeps the Longmont pill honest for the stories already
printed instead of inventing a value that claims a person chose it. The editor
sets the area at publish, on the publish step that is already there.

**No pill pressed is the whole paper** — which is the state the paper ships in
— and pressing one adds exactly one pressed pill and an "Everywhere" link back.
The prototype draws Longmont filled when nothing is chosen; that is a claim an
unfiltered page cannot back, so the deviation is deliberate.

## This week, and Dates in this story

Both panels read one thing: the dated records of **published** stories only. No
meeting calendar and no unpublished lead can reach either panel. The front page
asks for the next seven days on the paper's own calendar; the article page asks
for every date the one story carries, past and future. Both stop at twelve rows.
Usually the front-page panel is empty, and it says so in words rather than
printing a calendar this schema does not have.

## Dark mode is one warm black, on the desk and on the paper

Dark mode used to be pure black behind pure white. It is now a warm near-black
ground (`#1b1916`) with warm off-white text (`#e8e6e1`), and panels sit one step
up from the ground (`#27231f`) instead of blending into it. **The same pair is
used by the desk and by the public paper**, so switching between them no longer
looks like two different products.

## "Checked, nothing changed" no longer looks like "could not check"

The desk had collapsed the design's two state colors into one, so every
attention state painted the same red as a failure. Amber and red are now two
distinct colors in both themes, and the three notices carry three different
shapes, not three different words with the same border:

- **Could not check / failed** — a **2px dashed red** border. Dashed means the
  check did not finish.
- **Attention** (held, expiring soon, needs review) — a **2px solid amber**
  border.
- **Checked, nothing changed** — a **1px solid green** border.

## The type is Bricolage Grotesque and Literata, and it does not phone Google

Headlines and UI use Bricolage Grotesque; body text uses Literata. Both are now
**self-hosted** — 14 `woff2` files served from this app. No request to
`fonts.googleapis.com` leaves the app any more, so the desk and the paper render
in the intended faces on a machine with no internet, and nobody's reading habits
are reported to a third party.

## A job shows what it is doing, and can be stopped

A desk job used to show a spinner and one overwritten sentence. There was no
bar, no list of what the job would do, no way to tell a slow model from a dead
worker, and no way to stop a twenty-minute call. One card now renders all four
states:

- **Running** — the stage chips with the current one marked, a bar that moves
  when the job knows how far along it is and an indeterminate one when it does
  not, a `Now:` line, `Last activity m:ss ago`, and Cancel.
- **Stalled** — nothing heard from the worker for 60 seconds. A stall is not a
  failure, so Keep waiting, Retry on next model and Cancel are all still offered.
- **Done** — `Your draft is ready`, the bar filled, every chip ticked, and an
  Open button that goes where the server says the result landed.
- **Failed** — the provider's own reason, Retry, and Retry on another model.

**Cancel now reaches a call already in flight.** A twenty-minute model call has
no stage boundary inside it, so before this the editor's Cancel did nothing
until the model answered. The same 12-second tick that says
`Waiting on <model label> · 42s` now asks whether the editor has asked to stop
and abandons the wait. The provider call is not killed — nothing here can kill a
provider's socket — it is left to finish and its result discarded, and the
existing failover path is reused rather than duplicated.

A row with no heartbeat is **never** called stalled: null is "no evidence", not
"very quiet".

## One screen that answers "which model does what"

The desk could tell you whether a provider was signed in, but not which model
ran the daily scan, or the lead scoring, or a story draft. Those answers were
spread across three pickers that each asked a different question, and the answer
to "what happens when the first one fails" was nowhere at all.

**Models**, at `/desk/models`, is that screen. It opens from **Server
settings** ("Assign models to jobs →") and has two tabs.

**Who does what** is one row per job — the daily scan & lead filing, lead
scoring & duplicates, story drafting, the opinion, the evidence check, headline
suggestions, Dark Desk research, AI follow-ups, document reading & OCR, and
video & meeting transcripts. Each row carries a **First choice** with an
**effort** select beside it, then **Fallback 1** and **Fallback 2**, and a live
state chip: ✓ Ready, ! Slow, Sign-in expired, or Not built yet.

The desk tries the first choice, then Fallback 1, then Fallback 2, and records
which one actually ran. **A content refusal is final and never falls back** — if
a model reads the request and declines it, asking the next model the same
question would be shopping for a different answer, so the desk stops. **The
effort list is the exact model's**: a level the model does not declare is never
sent, which is what lets an assignment survive the model on a local server
changing. A setting the desk can no longer use says so, in amber, on its row,
rather than quietly running something else. **AI follow-ups** shows its row as
"Not built yet" instead of a picker, because the desk has no follow-up runner
yet — the row is the plan, not a setting.

**Connections** is the tab that used to be "Writing models", now in three
groups: **Frontier · API key**, **Subscription sign-ins (OAuth)**, and **Local &
self-hosted**, with every existing action kept. **There is no Load button and no
Pull button anywhere** — TownReporter reads what a server reports and never
tells it to load a model into anyone's GPU.

## 0.6.71's local-model picker ships live here

0.6.71 was published but never put live. Its change reaches the paper with this
release: choosing **Local model** now says which models are in memory
(`<id> · loaded`), sorts the loaded ones ahead inside each server group, offers
**Use whatever is loaded** as the first choice, and **stops before the call**
rather than asking a server for a model that is not loaded. The desk never
loads a model and never substitutes one silently.

## What a reader sees

A re-laid-out front page and article page, in the design's own type, with a
geography pill row that filters when pressed and two panels that name the dates
a story carries. Dark mode is a warm near-black rather than pure black. Nothing
else: no feed changes, and no existing article's saved model setting is
rewritten — a newsroom that never opens a picker or the Models screen keeps what
it already had.

## Migrations

Three additive migrations, and **they run on start**:

- **`0098_story_area.sql`** adds a nullable `area` tag to each printed story,
  which is what the geography pills filter on, plus an index over published
  stories.
- **`0099_desk_job_progress.sql`** adds the nullable progress columns to
  `desk_jobs` — the stage list, the current index, a percentage, the "now" step,
  a heartbeat, a cancel flag and the result's address — plus an index over
  running jobs.
- **`0100_model_assignments.sql`** creates the table holding up to three ranks
  per job per newsroom: the first choice and its two fallbacks.

## Compatibility

No existing behaviour is removed and nothing changes shape. Every change is
nullable, or creates a table that did not exist, or is a default that only adds
a value, so the old code runs against the new schema and everything a newsroom
has already stored reads as it did. A newsroom with no rows in
`model_assignments` behaves exactly as the desk did before.

**No new scheduled task, service, program, port or installer step.** The desk's
shared `Dialog` and `ChoiceCard` components are in the tree but no screen uses
them yet — moving the existing dialogs onto them is a later phase.

**Limits.** The redesign is not complete: the desk's own dialogs have not moved
onto the shared component yet, and the rest of the design system's component
vocabulary is not in the product. The dark theme's contrast was measured over
the desk's tokens in both themes, not over every screen. The `area` column, the
progress columns and the assignments table were exercised on PGlite with
migrations applied from disk; **PostgreSQL was not started and no production
database was contacted**. The migration-parity test that builds two real
postgres databases skips unless `TEST_POSTGRES_ADMIN_URL` is set. No browser was
opened for the job-progress card — it is asserted by rendering it to static
markup — and no person has looked at the paper's screenshots; every claim here
is a test result or a programmatic measurement.
