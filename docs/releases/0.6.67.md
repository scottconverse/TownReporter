This release is the **editor's controls**: a long machine-written to-do no longer
stops a story being saved or printed, the headline is a thing an editor can
plainly see they own and can rewrite even after the story has printed, and the
Publish button names the section the story will print under and confirms it in
the same press.

## A long to-do no longer blocks Save or Publish

A draft carried a to-do the desk itself had written — 227 characters against a
200-character limit — and pressing **Save** answered with a wall of raw JSON
(`too_big`, `maximum`, `todos,0,t`). Nothing was wrong with the story and
nothing was wrong with the to-do; the desk's reading of its own writing was the
only thing out of step.

- **The desk clips what it writes, cleanly.** Every path that writes a to-do —
  the scan, the redraft, the evidence pass — now clips to one shared bound at a
  word boundary and drops dangling punctuation, the way a sub-editor cuts a line
  rather than a way a parser truncates one. The desk's own gate reads the same
  bound, so the writer and the reader of a to-do can no longer disagree.
- **A stored to-do of any length can be saved back.** The round trip is
  normalised on the server rather than refused: text longer than the writer's
  bound is clipped the same clean way on the way in, so an item written before
  this release — or longer than any bound anyone sets — is never the reason a
  save fails. Storage keeps a sane ceiling; nothing an editor can type into the
  desk becomes uneditable.
- **A to-do that will not save cannot stop the printing.** Publishing saves the
  draft and the notes separately, and a failure in the notes is reported as a
  plain sentence beside a story that is nonetheless on the paper. The desk never
  refuses a valid draft because of its own notes.
- **No editor sees raw validation output again.** The story page reads field
  errors through one shared formatter that names the field in words ("the
  to-do list is longer than the desk can store") and says plainly that nothing
  was lost, on every surface that used to print the exception's own message.

## The headline is the editor's

- **It looks editable.** The headline box on a story page has a visible edge
  that strengthens on hover and takes the accent colour while the cursor is in
  it, with a small **Edit** hint, in both palettes and at AA contrast.
- **A redraft no longer silently replaces it.** The desk records which headline
  the model wrote, separately from the one on the row, and which of the two the
  editor last decided. Once an editor has changed the headline — or the
  headline differs from what the last model pass wrote — **Redraft** keeps the
  editor's words and files the model's new attempt beside them, not over them.
- **Two buttons, next to the box.** **Use the lead's headline** puts back the
  line the scan filed the lead under, in one press. **Suggest headlines** asks
  the story model, on the same provider ladder as everything else, for three
  options and shows them as choices; one click puts one in the box and nothing
  is applied without that click.
- **A printed headline can still be rewritten, from either screen.** A story on
  the paper can be re-headed on its own story page and on the **Published**
  page. The write changes the words only: the URL, and so every link to the
  story, is untouched. The desk records the old headline, the account and the
  time in an appended history table (`article_headline_history`, migration
  `0093_editor_headline_control.sql`) and in the action log. The public story
  page, the front page and the RSS feed all read the new headline on their next
  render. No correction notice is published for a headline change: the paper's
  correction rules never mentioned headlines, and a correction is for a story
  that was wrong.
- **A redraft does not count as an editor's headline.** An editor who only
  rewrote the body leaves the model's headline owned by the model, so the next
  redraft may still improve it.

## Publish names the section and confirms it in the same press

The separate **Confirm section** button and its warning are gone. In their
place:

- The publish button reads **Publish in <Section name>**, and a **change** link
  beside it puts the cursor in the section chooser. Pressing publish records
  the section confirmation for the exact draft version being printed, then
  publishes — one press, one decision, no second button to find first.
- When the scanner could not place the story and the section reads **Section not
  chosen — pick one**, publish is disabled until the editor picks one, with that
  reason printed beside the button.
- The server still refuses a draft whose section nobody confirmed. The publish
  request carries the section the editor was looking at; if that section is not
  the one on the draft, the desk refuses in a plain sentence rather than
  printing the wrong thing. A published story is confirmed for the version that
  printed, which is the guarantee the old button used to carry.
- Every time the section that printed differs from the section the model chose,
  the desk logs it — lead, the model's section, the editor's section and the
  time — and the **Stats** page counts them under **Section chosen by hand**.

## Compatibility

No existing behaviour is removed. A draft or lead written before this release
carries no recorded model headline or model section, and the desk says so
rather than guessing: it does not claim the editor wrote a headline it did not
record, and it does not count a story as a changed section when no model choice
was ever written down. One migration, `0093_editor_headline_control.sql`, and it
only adds columns and a table.
